## Curbside voting

If you have limited mobility, you can vote "curbside," outside the polling
place.[^sos-how-to-vote]

1. Go to your polling place.[^sos-how-to-vote]
2. Ask someone to go in and ask the poll workers to bring a ballot out to you.[^sos-how-to-vote]
3. Two election judges, one from each major party, bring you a ballot and what you need to
   vote. You must be within 200 feet of the polling place.[^rsmo-115-436]
4. You mark the ballot. The judges seal it in an envelope and put it in the ballot
   box.[^rsmo-115-436]

The poll workers should bring you a ballot within a reasonable time.[^sos-how-to-vote]

## Accessible voting equipment

Every polling place must have an accessible voting system for voters with
disabilities.[^sos-how-to-vote] It can read the ballot to you out loud, or make the text on
the screen bigger.[^sos-how-to-vote]

## A more accessible polling place

If you have a physical disability and your polling place is not accessible, you can ask the
county board for a different one.[^sos-how-to-vote][^rsmo-115-436] You can also vote at a
central polling place.[^sos-how-to-vote] Each local election office must have at least one central polling
place that is easy to get into for voters with physical disabilities and older voters.[^rsmo-115-115]
Call the county board at 314-615-1800 to ask.[^county-contact]

## Absentee ballots for voters with a permanent disability

If you have a permanent physical disability, you can ask to be put on a special list. Then
the county board mails you an absentee ballot application before each
election.[^sos-how-to-vote] See
[Voting with a disability, and getting help at the polls](/learn/help-voting).
